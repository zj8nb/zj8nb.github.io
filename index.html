Interactive Experience Research

ALANNN.COM

First Impression

Upon entering the site, the cartoon character on the landing page immediately caught my eye. This visual style felt playful compared to typical portfolio websites. I also noticed a cartoon fly that followed the cursor as it moved. It felt as though I had opened a game interface rather than just browsing a website; this compelled me to move the mouse and try clicking on elements that appeared interactive.

Two Minutes of Interaction

The power button on the character's chest stands out prominently. It looks exactly like the kind of button meant to be pressed, so I clicked it almost immediately. The character greeted me right away in response. This instant feedback makes the site feel more dynamic and encourages me to keep scrolling.

Two Minutes of Interaction

Exploring the Three Sections

During the first two minutes, most of my interactions involved moving the mouse, clicking on interactive elements, and scrolling. I began by moving my cursor across the landing page, noticing that the fly followed my movements; I then clicked the power button on the character's chest and watched the welcome animation.

About

Immediately afterward, a prompt appeared, guiding me to scroll. On a desktop, the page scrolled horizontally rather than vertically—an experience that instantly distinguished the navigation from that of traditional websites, where vertical scrolling is the norm. I continued scrolling through the main navigation, discovering three sections: "About," "Work," and "Links."

Work

During this brief interaction, the action I performed most frequently was swiping the screen, while most of my attention was focused on observing how the characters and the navigation interface responded to my actions.

Interaction & Navigation

Desktop vs Mobile

The website presents interactive content progressively rather than displaying all information at once. Elements such as on-screen characters, cursor-following components, and a power button invite users to engage with the site. Activating the power button triggers a welcome animation that provides clear visual feedback, setting the stage for the distinct visual styles and interaction methods found in subsequent sections.

A scroll prompt then guides users to the next phase of the experience. On desktop, the horizontal scrolling design creates the sensation of moving between different scenes.

Purpose of the Experience

The mobile version adapts this interaction model: instead of horizontal movement, content is arranged vertically, allowing users to navigate through sections via standard up-and-down swipes. This design preserves the site's original visual aesthetic while enhancing usability on touchscreen devices.
