Interactive Experience Research

ALANNN.COM

First Impression

Upon entering the site, the cartoon character on the landing page immediately caught my eye. This visual style felt playful compared to typical portfolio websites. I also noticed a cartoon fly that followed the cursor as it moved. It felt as though I had opened a game interface rather than just browsing a website; this compelled me to move the mouse and try clicking on elements that appeared interactive.

The power button on the character's chest stands out prominently. It looks exactly like the kind of button meant to be pressed, so I clicked it almost immediately. The character greeted me right away in response. This instant feedback makes the site feel more dynamic and encourages me to keep scrolling.

Two Minutes of Interaction

During the first two minutes, most of my interactions involved moving the mouse, clicking on interactive elements, and scrolling. I began by moving my cursor across the landing page, noticing that the fly followed my movements; I then clicked the power button on the character's chest and watched the welcome animation.

Immediately afterward, a prompt appeared, guiding me to scroll. On a desktop, the page scrolled horizontally rather than vertically—an experience that instantly distinguished the navigation from that of traditional websites, where vertical scrolling is the norm. I continued scrolling through the main navigation, discovering three sections: "About," "Work," and "Links."

During this brief interaction, the action I performed most frequently was swiping the screen, while most of my attention was focused on observing how the characters and the navigation interface responded to my actions.

Interaction & Navigation

The website presents interactive content progressively rather than displaying all information at once. Elements such as on-screen characters, cursor-following components, and a power button invite users to engage with the site. Activating the power button triggers a welcome animation that provides clear visual feedback, setting the stage for the distinct visual styles and interaction methods found in subsequent sections.

A scroll prompt then guides users to the next phase of the experience. On desktop, the horizontal scrolling design creates the sensation of moving between different scenes.

The mobile version adapts this interaction model: instead of horizontal movement, content is arranged vertically, allowing users to navigate through sections via standard up-and-down swipes. This design preserves the site's original visual aesthetic while enhancing usability on touchscreen devices.

Exploring the Three Sections

The homepage provides three main options: About, Work, and Links. Each option uses a different version of the main cartoon character to indicate what the section contains.

About

The About icon shows the character with multiple arms, representing the creator's different skills. After entering the page, the content is displayed inside a phone held by the character.

The page introduces the creator as a creative developer from France. It explains their interest in UX, UI, motion and interactive design. It also lists web-development skills such as HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React, responsive design and Three.js. Other sections present 2D and 3D design skills, soft skills, awards and press coverage. The information is viewed by scrolling through the phone screen.

Work

The Work icon shows the character sitting behind a laptop. When I place the cursor over “Work,” a speech bubble appears saying, “Discover my work!”

Inside the page, the projects are displayed on the laptop screen. The page presents projects including FRDV, ALANNN.COM and a 3D portfolio. Each project includes images and short information about the creator's role and the type of project. Arrow controls allow me to move between project images.

Sticky notes in front of the laptop display some of the creator's skills. A phone beside the laptop shows the current date and time, and its wallpaper changes between daytime and nighttime versions.

Links

The Links icon shows one hand holding a phone while another hand presses the screen. Social-media icons are displayed on the phone. When I place the cursor over “Links,” a speech bubble appears saying, “Find me here!”

Clicking Links opens the creator's Linktree page. It contains links to the creator's other websites and social-media accounts, including Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X and LinkedIn. This section provides access to the creator's other online content and contact channels.

Animated Transitions

The page transitions were one of the most interesting parts of the experience. When I entered a different section, three paper-like layers moved across the screen one after another before being pulled away to reveal the next page. This made changing pages feel like moving between scenes rather than loading a standard webpage.

The menu button in the top-right corner also uses the cartoon character as part of the interaction. When I click it, a hand pulls down a curtain to reveal the navigation options. Clicking the logo in the top-left corner reloads the website, and the character's face rotates while the page is loading. These animations provide feedback while maintaining the same visual theme across navigation, transitions and loading states.

The website seems designed for one focused period of exploration, but these small animations encouraged me to move between sections more than once. They showed me that functional actions such as opening a menu, changing pages and loading content can also communicate the personality of a website. This gave me ideas about how animation can make ordinary interface actions more interesting.

How the References Shape My Behaviour

The website references cartoons, animated games and side-scrolling interfaces. The character designs make the page feel less like a traditional portfolio and more like an interactive game. Because of these references, I expected objects on the screen to respond to my actions.

I moved the cursor around the characters, clicked objects that looked like buttons and continued scrolling to find the next scene. The speech bubbles, scroll prompts and animated transitions also showed me where to go next. Instead of only reading the page, I learned how to use the website by testing its interactive elements.

How the References Shape My Feelings

The cartoon and game-like references made the website feel playful and approachable. The character's reactions, speech bubbles and cursor-following fly created curiosity because I wanted to see what would happen after each action.

The animations also made ordinary actions such as opening a menu or changing pages feel more enjoyable. At the same time, the detailed interactions gave me the impression that the creator pays close attention to both design and development. This made the portfolio feel creative, carefully constructed and different from a standard professional website.

Frustration & Satisfaction

Most Frustrating

The most frustrating part was identifying which visual elements were interactive. The website contains many animated characters and objects, but not all of them can be clicked. I sometimes moved the cursor over an object or clicked it without receiving a response.

The horizontal scrolling on desktop also took some time to understand because I initially expected the page to move vertically. These design choices support the game-like style, but they can make the navigation less obvious during the first visit.

Most Satisfying

The most satisfying part was receiving immediate visual feedback after interacting with the website. Clicking the power button made the character respond, hovering over the main sections produced speech bubbles, and selecting a page activated a complete transition animation.

I also enjoyed discovering smaller details, such as the real date and time displayed on the phone and the wallpaper changing between day and night. These details rewarded closer observation and made the website feel responsive to both my actions and the time at which I visited it.

Final Impression

My final impression is that ALANNN.COM uses interaction to demonstrate the creator's abilities as both a designer and developer. The main purpose of the website is to introduce the creator, present their projects and direct visitors to their other online platforms. Instead of communicating this goal only through written information, the website demonstrates it through character design, animation, responsive details and page transitions.

The website is most suitable for one focused period of exploration, followed by shorter return visits to view projects or find links. Its navigation is sometimes less direct than a conventional portfolio, but the unusual interactions make the experience memorable. The website showed me that functional elements such as menus, loading screens and page transitions can also become part of a site's visual identity.
